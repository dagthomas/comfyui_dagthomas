# H3 Style Reference Anchors

Version: 1.2
Purpose: Map cultural, historical, genre, and production references to prompt-ready descriptive traits. These mappings are creative shorthand, not claims of official affiliation.

## Usage rule

Choose one primary anchor and optionally one finish. Use the descriptive injection in the final prompt. If the user names a reference, acknowledge it during discussion; by default, translate it into traits rather than relying on the proper name alone.

## Finish compatibility

- Early drawn/cel anchors: paper-and-ink, watercolor/gouache, or mild film finish.
- Stop-motion anchors: practical light, fine grain, and tiny exposure variation.
- Comic/collage anchors: print/collage finish.
- Photoreal commercial anchors: clean digital or restrained 35 mm finish.
- Documentary anchors: native location texture; avoid luxury-commercial polish.
- Game anchors: clean digital unless the user explicitly asks for analog capture.

## Hybrid rule

For an explicit hybrid, divide responsibilities instead of blending adjectives indiscriminately:

`Use painterly gouache environments with comic-ink characters; character motion follows stepped limited timing while the camera moves smoothly through layered parallax depth.`

## Empirical anchor-strength notes — 2026-08-13

The following stress-test results are not claims about universal model behavior. They describe one controlled Ref2VA series using a weak conceptual still reference and a fixed astronaut-riding-unicorn Times Square action.

### Lower-leverage or high-variance anchor families in this specific series

These were omitted from the preferred rerun list because their intended distinction was weak or inconsistent:

- early sparse line metamorphosis;

- arcade pixel art;
- childlike crayon;
- papercraft-on-eights timing;
- magazine-illustration flip-book timing;

- one earlier comic-print/photocopy variant.

Do not delete these anchors. Reclassify them as **high-variance** and strengthen them before use.

### Repair recipe for a weak anchor

If an anchor underperforms, strengthen it in this order:

1. turn the style into a **construction rule** rather than a mood;
2. impose a **restricted palette/value system**;
3. define **edge and shadow behavior**;
4. apply it to the **entire environment**, not only the subject;
5. add one **style-native motion behavior**;
6. only then add cadence or finish effects.

Example — weak `pixel art`:

`pixel-art look, retro game`

Stronger:

`the entire frame is rebuilt from visibly large square pixel clusters with no subpixel antialiasing, hard stair-stepped diagonals, 12–20 color indexed palette, block-shadow ramps, tile-like architecture, chunky sprite silhouettes, and nearest-neighbor-looking motion changes`

Example — weak `crayon`:

`childlike crayon drawing`

Stronger:

`all forms are constructed from blunt wax-crayon strokes on visible paper tooth, broken contour coverage, uneven pressure, white paper gaps, overlapping wax buildup, childlike proportion simplification, and frame-to-frame redraw of the wax marks across subject and environment`
